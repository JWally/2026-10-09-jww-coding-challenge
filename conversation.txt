[2026-10-09 12:34:18 CDT] CLAUDE:
Yes, macOS has one built in, the Screenshot toolbar:

1. Press **⌘ Shift 5**.
2. In the toolbar, choose **Record Entire Screen** or **Record Selected Portion**.
3. Open **Options** and pick your mic under **Microphone**, e.g. "MacBook Pro Microphone". This is what records your narration. It's set to None by default, so it's easy to miss.
4. In the same menu, choose where to save the file (Desktop by default). You can also set a timer, or turn on **Show Mouse Clicks**, which is handy for demos.
5. Click **Record**. To stop, click the ⏹ button in the menu bar or press **⌘ Control Esc**.

You get a `.mov` file. For quick edits, open it in QuickTime Player and use **Edit → Trim** (⌘T).

**QuickTime Player → File → New Screen Recording** opens the same toolbar.

As far as I know, the built-in tool can't record the Mac's own sound, like app audio or a video playing. It only captures the microphone. For that you'd need a third-party tool such as OBS (free) or a virtual audio device like BlackHole. Your voice over a screen recording works fine without anything extra.
